1

No charter can buy its second branch with what it earns

Every one of the 1,000 Founding Charters opens with exactly one branch. Tokens are minted only at a withdrawal, so at genesis every wallet is empty. Retiring a branch is the only withdrawal, and retiring one branch out of one liquidates the whole balance and burns the charter.

That closes both internal funding routes at once. The second branch has to be paid for with $STANDARD bought on the open market, which means ETH from outside the earning loop. This is arithmetic from sections 3, 6, 7 and 9, not a simulation result.

What the simulation adds is the size of the effect. Move the share of charters willing to buy on the open market and the whole system resolves to one line.

Across all 900 runs the resting branch count tracks 1,000 + 9,000 × participation, with a maximum deviation of 2.3%. The deviation is never a charter escaping one branch. It comes from charters already holding two or more, which can self-finance once the auction is sitting at its floor.

2

The burn engine has a computable expiry date

The whitepaper calls expansion the protocol's largest supply sink, and it is. It also has a hard ceiling nobody has priced: 9,000 expandable slots against a supply of 100 licenses a day. Ninety days, and the engine is done.

Burn peaks near three times daily issuance in the first week, then decays as each new branch dilutes the yield that sets the license floor. At saturation it reaches exactly zero, in 900 runs out of 900.

Charter auctions are the only thing that restarts the engine, and they restart it faintly. At twenty-five new charters a day the burn settles near a tenth of daily issuance and is still falling, against a peak above three times. Raise the cadence and see for yourself.

3

Two identical branches, a hundredfold difference in what they cost

Section 8 lets each day open at twice the previous close, and describes the result as roughly a hundredfold move in a week. That is accurate. What it does not say is what the week costs the people buying inside it.

A license bought on the third or fifth day takes over two hundred days of branch yield to repay. The same license, granting the same branch, bought near saturation repays in two. Nothing about the asset differs. Only the day.

This is the one finding whose magnitude depends on behaviour rather than structure. At the full participation this panel shows, the spread runs from about 51× at a ten day horizon to about 226× at sixty. Across all 900 runs, which include thinner participation, the median is 52× and the floor is 8×. The existence of the effect does not depend on any of that. The escalation rule that produces it is in section 8.

5

What is measured, assumed, and left out

Why the redacted parameters do not matter

The license floor is defined in section 7 as roughly two days of one branch's yield, and one branch's yield is daily issuance divided by branch count. Both sides carry the same base rate and policy multiplier, so the ratio of daily burn to daily issuance reduces to 200k/N, where k is how far above the floor the auction clears. Findings 1 and 2 are stated entirely in that ratio. No redacted value enters them.

Assumptions, all adjustable in the code

  • Resolution fee floor 5%, ceiling 50%, saturating at 20% weekly exit pressure. The ceiling was swept from 30% to 70% with no change to any finding.
  • Buyer willingness to pay is a payback horizon drawn from a normal distribution, mean adjustable above, standard deviation swept from 5 to 25 days.
  • The continuous Dutch descent is approximated by uniform clearing at the price where cumulative demand meets the daily supply.

Not modelled

  • Any price for $STANDARD. Everything is denominated in tokens and in days of yield.
  • The contraction vault, net flow regimes, and the policy multiplier. Findings 1 and 2 are invariant to the multiplier by construction; finding 3 would move with it.
  • Secondary charter transfers, which section 12 leaves for later.
  • Dormancy revocation, which returns branches to the pool of the living and would slightly extend saturation.

Built from whitepaper v0.1 while the protocol is pre-launch. There is no token, no NFT, and no chain data to check any of this against yet, which is exactly why it is worth writing the model down now rather than after.
